Satellite imagery obtained by Bellingcat lays bare the extent of tree-felling at a Washington DC golf course that President Trump is reportedly seeking to transform into a championship-level course, despite moves to protect the site by conservationists. 

The extent and circumstances surrounding the rapid changes prompted a judge to call an emergency hearing earlier this week, banning any further changes until later this month.

Reports on Wednesday detailed that 430 trees were cut down at the East Potomac Golf Links  which is owned by the Federal government and managed by the National Park Service (NPS). This is despite a judge reportedly stating earlier this year that no more than 10 trees at the course could be cut down without first notifying the court. 

NPR reported that officials notified the court just before 5pm on Friday Oct. 9 of plans to remove up to 529 trees in phases starting  the next day on Saturday Oct. 10. That prompted US District Court Judge Ana Reyes to call an emergency hearing on Oct. 12. By that time, 430 trees had reportedly been cut down.

Reyes is reported to have said she was “furious” with the Justice Department for the lack of time afforded the court and plaintiffs before the tree removal was carried out.

She was also reported to have said that the government’s actions had cost it “all of its credibility with me in this case.”

Images taken at the course earlier this week show trunks and branches strewn out across fairways.

Reuters reported that an organisation called The ​DC Preservation League said in a February ​2026 lawsuit that President Donald Trump was flouting ⁠environmental laws to “irreparably destroy a priceless piece of American history” so he could “convert a public recreation space into an exclusive, high-end golf course.”

Kevin Griess, an NPS official, reportedly stated at Monday’s hearing that tree removal was part of a 2019 plan that predates Trump’s aims for the course and sought to restore links-style vegetation.

Bellingcat contacted NPS as well as the Department of Justice but did not receive a response before publication.

A golfer hits toward felled trees at the East Potomac Golf Links. REUTERS/Kevin Lamarque

The changes to the golf course are not the only ones President Trump has sought in Washington DC and the surrounding area since the beginning of his second term. In October last year  demolition began on the East Wing of the White House with the aim of building a new ballroom claiming it was “the safest building ever built.”  

Trump has also proposed a 250-foot-tall “Triumphal Arch” to be built just outside the Arlington National Cemetery in Virginia.
